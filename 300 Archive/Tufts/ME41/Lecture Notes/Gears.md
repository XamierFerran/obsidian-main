Definition - a rotating machine part that is typically used to change the speed, torque, and/or direction of a power source.
- Most commonly, a gear is meshed (mated) with another gear to produce the desired output. When meshed with a non-rotating rack, translational output is created

### Types of Gears
- Spur Gears
	- Teeth are parallel to the axis of rotation
	- Used to transmit motion from one shaft to a parallel shaft.
	- ![[Pasted image 20221107024055.png]]
	- ![[Pasted image 20221107024325.png]]
- Helical Gears
	- Teeth are inclined to the axis of rotation
	- Used for the same applications as spur gears, but not as noisy (gradual engagement of teeth)
	- Inclined tooth develops thrust lods and bending couples, not present in spur gears
	- Sometimes used to transmit torque between nonparallel shafts.
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107024250.png]]
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107024306.png]]
- Bevel Gears
	- Teeth are formed on conical surfaces and used msotly for transmitting motion between intersecting shafts
	- can have straight tooth, spiral (tooth is not straight but forms circular arc), or hypoid bevel gears (tooth forms circular arc but shafts are non-intersecting)
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107024523.png]]
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107024548.png]]
- Worm Gears
	- Resembles a screw
	- Direction of rotation depends on worm rotation and if teeth are right handed or left handed
	- Teeth of one or both may wrap partly around the other (single or double enveloping)
	- Mostly sued when required speed ratio is high and or when torque needs to increase.
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107024751.png]]

### Gears - Basic Theory
- Spur Gears - Nomenclature
	- Pitch Circle: is theoretical circle that calculationsa re based on (meshing pairs of gears have tangent pitch circles)
	- Pitch Diameter: is the diameter of the pitch circle
	- Addendum & Dedendum are radial distances for top and bottom land
	- Clearance c is amount by which dedendum exceeds addendum (avoid top land interference)
	- Typically, a smaller spur hear (the "pinion") drives a larger spur egear (the "gear")
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107025127.png]]
	- Circular Pitch p is the distance, measured on pitch circle, from point on one tooth to corresponding point on adjacent tooth
		- $p = \frac{\pi *d}{N}=\pi *m$
	- Module m is ratio of pitch diameter to number of teeth (SI system)
		- $m = \frac{d}{N}$
	- Diametral P is ratio of number of teeth on a gear to the pitch diameter (english system)
		- $P = \frac{N}{d}$
	- ![[Pasted image 20221107025719.png]]
- Spur Gears - Standard Sizes
	- Standards developed by the American Gear Manufacturers Association (AGMA)
	- Can ensure interchangeability and constrain available geometries to a manageable selection
	- In English system, there are "coarse" and "fine" pitch sizes available
	- In SI system, categorized as "preferred" or "next choice"
	  ![[Pasted image 20221107030041.png]]
- Spur Gears - Line of Action
	- Spur gear teeth are deisgned to produce a constant angular velocity ratio - a phenomenon known as "conjugate action"
	- The most common conjugate profile is the involute profile, which can be visualized by unwrapping a string from a cylinder, keeping the string taut and tangent to the cylinder
	- The point of contact between meshing teeth represents tangency of the teeth surfaces
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107212336.png]] ![[Pasted image 20221107212359.png]]
		- angular velocity ratio: $|\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2}| = \frac{r_2}{r_1}$
			- $\omega$ is angular velocity (rad/s)
			- $r$ is pitch radius (in or mm)
	- Good Conjegate Action Guarantees:
		- Tooth-on-tooth contact traces an inclinded line
		- Line is known as **Pressure Line** or **Line of Action**
	- Forces transmitted along the line of action
		- ![[Pasted image 20221107212919.png]]
- Spur Gears - Pressure Angle
	- The pressure line/line-of-action is manufactured at standard pressure angle $\phi$
	- Standards pressure angles suggested by AGMA are $20, 22^{\frac{1}{2}}, 25$ degrees.
