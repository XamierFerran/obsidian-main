The reading by Wickens and Hollands serves the purpose of explaining how information theory quantifies information and maps it to probabilities and other statistical tools. I find defining information as the reduction of uncertainty to be a very clever idea as it decently justifies Wickens and Hollands' attempt to enumerate information. That being said, while the definition encapsulates most packets of information, there are definitely a wide variety of edge cases that cannot fall neatly into the definition of information according to this paper. The paper also talks about specific short comings such as not accounting for correctness or magnitude of error. 
Camden's paper pushes quantifiable representation further by exploring concepts such as mutual information and entropy to describe multitasking. I think their findings strongly relate to the information overload of a digitized and globalized world where everything is connected at all times. With an overloading of information, the effort to stay on task and complete really anything becomes much more difficult. Personally, I find myself limiting my information intake to curated and intentional streams of information so I can focus on what I want to truly prioritize in life.
