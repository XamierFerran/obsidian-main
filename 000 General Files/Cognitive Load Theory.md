[[On the Nature of Vigilance]]
[[Cognitive Load Theory]]

Hancock (2017) and Sweller (2011) both explore the limits of human attention, but from different angles. Hancock reframes vigilance as something we do strategically, not passively. Sweller, through Cognitive Load Theory, highlights the limited capacity of working memory and how easily it can be overloaded by poor design or too much complexity.

In today’s world of constant digital distraction, their insights feel more relevant than ever. The presence of multiple devices (phones, laptops, watches) all competing for our attention fries a person's attention span. Instead of supporting deep focus or learning, this environment overwhelms our mental bandwidth. We’re constantly switching tasks, monitoring notifications, and processing shallow content, leaving little space for sustained thinking or meaningful engagement. It’s a setup that both depletes vigilance and overloads our cognitive systems.
