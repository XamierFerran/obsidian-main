# Theory of technological politics
>One strength of this point of view is that it takes technical artifacts seriously.
# Two ways artifacts can contain political power
1. instances in which the invention,design, or arrangement of a specific technical device or system becomes a way of settling an issue in a particular community.
2. Cases of what can be called inherently political technologies, man-made systems that appear to require, or to be strongly comparable with, particular kinds of political relationships
# Technical Arrangements as Forms of Order
Anecdote of 1920s to 1970s New York City builder, Robert Moses, builds overpasses super low to keep buses out of his neighborhoods. Thus reflecting on his social-class bias and racial bias. This was an intentional and known decision that was lost over time until it was simply baked into the land. Winner provides multiple anecdotes to further back his point of there existing intentional technological design that in-fact does not prioritize efficiency but instead prioritizes dividing people even if they lose some profit.
