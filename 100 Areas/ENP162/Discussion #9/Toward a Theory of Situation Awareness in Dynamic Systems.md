## Introduction
- Endsley strives to show 
	1. The importance of situational awareness (SA) in decision making in dynamic environments and the utility of using a middle of decision making that takes AS into account, and
	2. a theory of SA that expands on prior work in that area
- He also goes on to explain that SA is not a simple concept where a user must be aware of multiple pieces of data, it is multidimensional
- The need for SA applies in a wide variety of environments, and becomes more difficult as the situation becomes more complex and dynamic and time bound
## A Model of Situational Awareness
The goal of this section is to establish a common ground for future discussion regarding SA as, at the time of publish of this article, there is not much research on SA and it would be difficult to define a a rigorous theory at that point.
### A Model
![[Pasted image 20250222181338.png]]
### Level 1 SA: Perception of the Elements in the Environment
User must perceive the status, attributes and dynamics of relevant elements int he environment
### Level 2 SA: Comprehension of the Current Situation
Take the disjointed elements of level 1 SA and consider them together in light of operator goals, basically forming a holistic picture of the environment
### Level 3 SA: Projections of Future Status
Ability to project the future actions of the elements in the environment is achieved through knowledge of the status and dynamics of the elements and comprehension of the situation

Overall, these levels go to show that situational awareness is not just knowing the elements but compounded information to understand the elements in their current context and predict how these elements may change next

### Elements
In any context where situation awareness is relevant, it is not justifiable to try to paint a broad stroke of elements across a variety of different contexts
### Time
Situational Awareness is highly time based as it often takes time to be able to build up a reservoir of knowledge and time to contextualize them etc.

## Summary
- a person's SA is restricted by limited attention and working memory capacity
