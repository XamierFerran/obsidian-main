```ad-question
How does situation awareness work differently between experts (e.g. pilots) and non-experts (e.g. everyday drivers)? Are they doing the same things? can situation awareness be measured the same ways?
```
Using Endsley's framework for situational awareness, I do not think that there is a difference between situation awareness between experts and non-experts except that experts might have more situation awareness do to prior experience. This is because Endsley's framework is general enough to apply across varying levels of situation awareness, thus acting as a tool to describe the experience of a variety of different experience levels.
SAGAT, while being useful as a method of evaluating situational awareness at all, is limiting in its realism of extracted information. Due to it being a direct interruption to the driver's experience that would never actually happen in a real life scenario, it is not super contextually applicable source of data. 
Daze works better in my opinion because it is more realistic to the driving experience. I think using a Waze inspired interface is incredibly clever as it already provides valid data acquisition of a situationally aware driver. As someone who has used Waze before, I think the interactions are quick and seamless, and I agree that it can be a useful comparison tool for both simulation and IRL testing since it is a generally safe way to acquire data.
