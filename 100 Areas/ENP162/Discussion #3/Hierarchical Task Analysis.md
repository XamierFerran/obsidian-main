Process of **HTA** is to decompose tasks into sub tasks. Sub tasks are defined by a goal, the input conditions needed for the goal, actions required to attain goal, and the feedback indicating goal attainment.

## Origins of HTA
In the 1950's there was new technology in both industry and military settings. Automation decreased physical effort and increased mental effort. Classical methods (Gilbreth's motion study) that had been used for half a century were inadequate. 
## Analysis Versus Description
Analysis is not description. **HTA** is not just listing out tasks, sub tasks, goals, etc. Analysis is a procedure aimed at identifying performance problems (sources of error) and proposing solutions.
## Tasks and Goals
**HTA** differs from earlier methods of analysis by beginning, not with a list of activities, but by identifying the goals of each task. It is important to attach goal to task to contextualize each task and identify if it is accomplishing its purpose related to the goal. Goals can be **active or latent** depending on conditions that arise.
## Decomposition and Redescription
Goals can be complex, and can be defined by more than one event or variables. Breaking the goal down into component states is **the process of decomposition**.
### Two types of decomposition
1. Identifying goal states specified by multiple criteria e.g. arrive at a destination a) with minimum effort b) with no injury
2. Identifying goal states with sub goals that creates a route for achieving the overall goal state. It is a nested hierarchy
Decomposition (a.k.a redescription) is a great way to locate sources of general system error in failure to attain specific sub goals.
## Operations: Input, Action, and Feedback
**Operation**: is a fundamental unit of analysis specified by a goal, the circumstances in which the goal is activated (the **Input**), the **actions** that contribute to goal attainment, and the conditions indicating goal attainment (**Feedback**).
- Operations can be **decomposed** the same way goals can. By listing out sub operations and their components you should be able to collectively comprise an exhaustive statement of sub goals.
## Plans
A **plan** can be defined as "a fixed sequence or routine procedure" or another common type specifies "a selective rule or decision" like an if-statement. These imply knowledge on the part of the operator. Thus the requirement of **cognitive task analysis**.
Another type of **plan** requires two or more **operations** be pursued in parallel. In other words, the **superordinate goal** cannot be attained unless two or more **subordinate goals** care attained at the same time. This is **time-sharing or dual task plan**. **Plans** have the purpose of deciding when certain **goals** become **active** while others remain **latent**. In summary
### Three types of common plans
1. a fixed sequence or routine procedure
2. a selective rule or decision
3. A requirement of two or more operations pursued in parallel
## Stop Rules
**Stop rules** specify the level of detail needed for decomposition until no more is needed. The ultimate stop rule is 
	"stop when you have all the information you need to meet the purposes of analysis." 
A general stop rule is 
	"stop when the product of the probability of failure and the cost of failure are judged as acceptable"
This is known as the p x c criterion. It is important to know that this calculation can vary in complexity depending on if it is solely a system problem or if this involves operators such as training.
