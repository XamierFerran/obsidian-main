## What is automation?
- execution of an action by a machine that was originally done by a human. 
- "Today's automation could very well be tomorrow's machine"
- The spectrum:
	- Full manual - human control
	- Full automation - full machine control
## Human roles in automated systems
- Automation is usually applied when it can replace a human at a lower cost
- Level of automation reflects the levels of trust in the designer and the operator
- Designers tend to automate as much as they can and leave the rest for the operator but the leads to many human factors problems
## Incidents and accidents
- Human performance issues have not been able to be fully addressed through design or training considering the above information
- Overreliance and underreliance are a common theme: How do they decide when or how to use the automation
## Overview
- Human use us including operators, designers, supervisors, managers, and regulators
- Misuse = overreliance
- Disuse = underutilization
- Abuse = inappropriate application by designers or managers
## Use of automation
- Automation usage and attitude are correlated
## Mental workload
- You would think that having a higher mental workload would make an operator want to use the automation, but through different studies of varying automation complexity its found that they are not correlated
## Cognitive overhead
- When the benefit is not immediately clear, the operator might not choose to use the automation simply because it is too hard to find the application
