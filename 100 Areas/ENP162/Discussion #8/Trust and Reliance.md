```ad-question
How do you see the similarities between human-human trust and reliance and human-machine trust and reliance?
```
Lee and See's paper initially draws from literature in the field to show that  that trust in a human to human relationship is similar in functionality to how trust in a human automation relationship should work. And this seems to work mostly because the automation, if designed correctly, should have the goal of engaging a certain amount of trust from the operator/human. In this way, the designer might pull from human to human trust relationships to influence how the human might change their feelings of trust towards the machine.
Trust isn't necessarily something that is always needed in human machine relationship if the environment and interaction is simple enough. In this case, the operator might just have a good understanding of what to do next. In which case, according to Hoff and Bashir, this is actually still trust just a learned trust or a trust that comes more from understanding than faith. In this article, the authors argue that trust is specific to when the situation and machine application is complex so the user must have a situational or faithful trust to keep working. In this way it is useful because it allows users to keep trust and efficient use even when things change in complexity.
After reading both articles, I have a personal belief that, despite the similarities between human to human trust and reliance versus human to machine trust and reliance, human to human trust cannot be replicated by an automated machine. I think it can achieve a flattened version of trust that does not rival in the complexity of a human to human relationship. However, I do not think this is very relevant to the trust and reliance discussion as from what I can see from the papers, the goal isn't to recreate human to human trust and reliance, it is to create trust and reliance at all and how to define and manipulate it.
