## Abstract
In this paper, Hoff and Bashir look at research papers that include empirical data regarding trust in human-automation systems to synthesize a three layer trust model that encapsulates human-automation trust: dispositional trust, situational trust, and learned trust.
## Automation
Human trust in a system has a direct correlation with failures in use such as misuse, disuse, and abuse.
## Trust
Three commonalities across trust in different research fields
- Truster that gives trust
- Trustee that accepts trust
- Something at stake
Trustee must have some incentive to perform a task, and there must be a possibility that the trustee fails.
## Defining Trust
Trust is always grounded in at least one quality of the trustee: ability, integrity, benevolence. Trust formation is very dynamic. In human relationships it is based on very quick almost immediate pattern recognition and analysis. For humans interacting with machines, the same can be said but where the trustee is one stepped removed as they think about the designer instead.
## Distinctions Between Human-Automation and Interpersonal Trust
Human-automation trust depends on attributes such as performance, process, and purpose. Basically, operators trust a machine based on how well they perceive it working.
Progression is flipped compared to human-human trust. Usually it starts out with prediction, then dependability, and then faith. For machines, humans tend to have a belief that machines are perfect off of faith, then it rapidly deteriorates as it goes to dependability and finally predictability.
## Empirical Research on Factors That Influence Trust
Broad sources of variability in trust:
1. human operator
2. environment
3. automated machine
Which directly corelate with:
1. dispositional trust
2. situational trust
3. learned trust
