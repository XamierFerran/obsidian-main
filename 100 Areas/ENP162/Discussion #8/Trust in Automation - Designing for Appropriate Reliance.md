## Introduction
So far, this paper goes on to suggest that trust in a human to human relationship is similar in functionality to how trust in a human automation relationship should work. And this seems to work mostly because the automation, if designed correctly, should have the goal of engaging a certain amount of trust from the operator/human. In this way, the designer might pull from human to human trust relationships to influence how the human might change their feelings of trust towards the machine.

Trust isn't necessarily something that is always needed in human machine relationship if the environment and interaction is simple enough. In this case, the operator might just have a good understanding of what to do next. In which case, according to Hoff and Bashir, this is actually still trust just a learned trust or a trust that comes more from understanding than faith. In this article, the authors argue that trust is specific to when the situation and machine application is complex so the user must have a situational or faithful trust to keep working. In this way it is useful because it allows users to keep trust and efficient use even when things change in complexity.
