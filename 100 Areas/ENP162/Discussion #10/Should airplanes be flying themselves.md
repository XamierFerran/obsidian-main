## I. Into the Night
- It seems systemically, the situation was great, great airline, great pilots, great team, great plane, but there was an information glitch for a short duration that overwhelmed the pilots
- To some extent, the problem was automation. Skill atrophy. The pilots never had to face a raw crisis before
- One of the pilots got one hour of sleep the night before
- The conversations in the cockpit were regular conversations that included natural human emotion nervousness and casualness and light implicit pressure. Nothing out of the ordinary but if conversations went differently, they might be alive. Specifically if the captain didn't decide to sleep right before going into the storm.
## II. Cockpit Resource Management
- Jet airplanes introduced dramatically greater safety in the 1950s, and opened the way to airline travel as we know it today.
- With this, it was revealed that all of the few crashes at this point were pilot failures, which is interesting since before this point pilots were basically what stood between passengers and mechanical/weather failures
- Pilots got their salaries cut because of this improvement in technology
- After implementing a flight simulator, it was found that one of the key features of a good flight is good team coordination and how the captain treated the crew. When the captain put on pressure or ignored people they made dangerous mistakes.
- Crew Resource Management: less authoritarian cockpit culture
